Goal

The Phoenix project aims at supporting researchers to become aware of the energy footprint of their data and learn how they can adopt habits of sustainable data management, so as to both optimize the existing data and change their own behavior.

Motivation

We focus on data unsustainability for two reasons. First, data is a digital asset shared by industry and research, so we can learn from industry results and adapt them to academia, and vice versa. Second, in both contexts the way data is managed has a large and growing environmental impact worldwide.

Most data that organisations store is never used. The Veritas study estimates that only 15% of organisational data is business critical, 33% is redundant, obsolete or trivial, and 52% is unidentified, yet all of it keeps consuming energy. Similar figures apply to research. IDC also expects the worldwide installed storage capacity to exceed 19 ZB by 2028, up from 9.3 ZB in 2023[2], and the growth of AI investments is accelerating these trends.

Method

We integrate two existing methods, and the knowledge gathered by applying them in industry, and transfer them to academic research.

SIIA: Sustainability IT Impact Assessment

Defined by NCDD, the SIIA grew out of industrial practice. It guides people and organisations who want to start investing in sustainable IT. It consists of a guide, a wiki of tactics collected in industrial use cases, and the SIIA radar, a visual overview for selecting and prioritising those tactics. Practitioners find it easy to understand, pragmatic and visually appealing, but its tactics are informal.

SAF Toolkit: Sustainability Assessment Framework

Built on rigorous academic research and over a decade of case studies, the SAF Toolkit[5] helps software architects and IT decision makers model sustainability as a software quality property. It offers a database of reusable sustainability-quality (SQ) concerns, an open source archive of tactics, a checklist and a decision graph. Its three main outputs are an SQ Model, a Decision Map and a KPI Model.

Better together

The SIIA can be the visual front end that guides researchers in planning for sustainability. The SAF Toolkit can be the rigorous back end that translates the plan into concrete tactics and measures their effect, with full traceability from IT features to indicators.

Example: cleaning up research datasets

A research dataset should typically be kept for 10 years in a persistent repository. In practice, copies often stay behind on unprotected platforms such as Google Drive, or are kept for the wrong period. A tactic could give each dataset an expiration date, for semi-automated cleaning after 10 years, and remove duplicates. Its impact is measured as the amount of unused data cleaned up and duplicate data removed, and the energy this saves.

Work plan

Once the methods are integrated, we carry out 2–3 case studies with researchers from NCDD member institutions. Each case study picks a research data management (RDM) use case, models its IT features and sustainability concerns, selects and applies energy-saving tactics, and measures the effect on energy consumption and carbon emissions. Use cases are chosen for the scope and size of their data, the availability of researchers and IT support, and whether the effect of a tactic can be measured.

  1. WP1: Integrate and adapt existing methods

    Integrate the SAF Toolkit instruments into the SIIA. A first version of the integrated method is ready in the first quarter, then updated every quarter with case study results.

    M1: SIIA and SAF Toolkit integrated into one method, from practice to research.

  2. WP2.1: Validate the integrated method

    Apply and improve the method in 2–3 case studies. We start at VU Amsterdam with the VU Sustainability Office. Other candidates are TU Delft, University of Twente, Hogeschool Utrecht, Radboud University and Leiden University.

    M2: Case studies completed and the method revised.

  3. WP2.2: Validate existing and create new knowledge

    Model and measure green tactics that reduce the energy use and carbon footprint of each use case. See also the S2 group's Awesome and Dark Tactics.

    M3: Existing tactics validated and new ones created.

  4. WP3: Knowledge dissemination and reporting

    A public event for NCDD members, Amsterdam Sustainability Institute (ASI) members and partners such as SURF, NL-RSE and GreenLabs NL. We also prepare training material so researchers can learn about their options for sustainable data management.

    M4: Dissemination event and paper draft.

Potential impact

According to Statistics Netherlands (CBS), the 45 or so commercial data centres in the Netherlands accounted for 4.6% of national energy use in 2024[6]. The country has some 200 data centres in total. About 75% of them are attached to universities, hospitals and industry and used for in-house data processing, and are not included in that figure.

If a large share of the data stored in data centres is unnecessary, as in the 85% found for enterprises, the project can contribute substantially to energy optimisation. The aim is that future research produces data that is sustainable by design.

Open science and dissemination

The integrated method and all knowledge produced by the project are shared as open source and open access. The SIIA wiki and the SAF Toolkit repositories are already open source. At the end of each quarter, NCDD and VU partners consolidate results into one open source platform, for example a new GitHub repository.

VU Amsterdam ensures that all case study activities follow open science principles: transparency, reproducibility and equity. ASI publishes results on a dedicated webpage and on LinkedIn, and shares them through sustainable science networks such as the Sustainable Development Solutions Network and the International Sustainable Campus Network, to reach beyond the Netherlands.
